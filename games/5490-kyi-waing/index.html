Kyi waing

Also known as ကြေးဝိုင်း · Burmese gong circle

The kyi waing is a ring of kettle gongs, often twenty or more, hung on cords inside a low carved circular frame. A single player sits in the middle and plays quick, glittering tunes with two small beaters.

How to play

  1. A ring of gongs is lit; the lowest sits at the front left and the pitch climbs round the circle.
  2. Strike each gong as its ring closes. The beater visits it and the bronze answers.
  3. Runs go round the circle, so keep your eyes moving.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Each pattern repeats twice.
  • The round ends when the last stroke has been judged; the score is the share of the best possible.

About the tradition

The gong circle is tuned note by note, small gongs for the high notes and larger ones for the low. Its frame is lacquered red and gilded.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Classical Worlds (about 1000 BCE to 500 CE), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Southeast Asia and the Islands.

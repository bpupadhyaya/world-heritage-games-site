Ghatam

Also known as கடம்

The ghatam is a clay pot, thick-walled and burnished, used as a drum in the music of southern India. Held mouth to the belly and struck with the fingers, palms and the heel of the hand, it gives a ringing metallic note on the shoulder, a woody tone on the belly and a deep boom when the mouth is covered.

How to play

  1. The pot is lit in five places: the mouth for a deep Gumki, the neck for a light Nam tap, the shoulder for a ringing Tha, the belly for a Thom and the base for a low note.
  2. A ring closes on the place to strike. Strike as it lands.
  3. The shoulder rings brightest and the mouth sounds deepest.
  4. Say the names aloud in time as you play.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Each pattern repeats twice.
  • The patterns count four beats to the bar, with a fast run at the end.

About the tradition

The ghatam is made by potters from clay mixed with brass or iron filings, then fired hard. Players in southern Indian ensembles tune each pot to the singer and speak its strokes as syllables.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of South Asia.

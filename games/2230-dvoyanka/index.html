Dvoyanka

The dvoyanka is a Bulgarian double flute: two pipes in one block with a single beak, one carrying the melody with finger holes and the other sounding beside it. The wood is pale and burnt with red zigzags, and the two voices blend into a soft, doubled tone.

How to play

  1. A covered hole glows; an open hole shows its dark bore. Seven notes of the mode are lit down the melody pipe on the left.
  2. Each strike shows the fingering of that note and a band of breath runs from the beak to it.
  3. The second pipe sounds along with the melody.
Rules at a glance
  • A phrase is a line of notes, each struck close to its beat.
  • Long notes swell slowly: strike on the beat and let them ring.
  • The round ends when the last note has been judged; the score is the share of the best possible.

About the tradition

It is a shepherd's and village instrument, played for dances and for company.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Classical Worlds (about 1000 BCE to 500 CE), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Northern and Eastern Europe.

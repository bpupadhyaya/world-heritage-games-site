Mangala

Also known as Mangola · Turkish mancala

Six round pits and a long treasury for each player, four stones in every pit. A last stone that makes a rival pit even takes it, and one that lands in an empty pit of yours takes the pit facing it.

How to play

  1. Lift all the stones from one of your pits; the first goes back into that pit and the rest are dropped one by one, counter-clockwise.
  2. Your own treasury takes a stone each time you pass it; the rival's treasury is skipped.
  3. If your last stone makes a rival pit hold an even number, take that pit.
  4. If your last stone falls in an empty pit of yours, take it together with the rival pit facing it.
  5. If your last stone lands in your treasury, play again.
Rules at a glance
  • Forty-eight stones, four in each of twelve pits.
  • A pit with several stones puts the first stone back into itself, then sows on.
  • Your own treasury receives stones as you pass it; the rival's treasury never does.
  • A last stone that makes an opposing pit even sends all its stones to your treasury.
  • A last stone in an empty pit of yours takes the facing pit and your own stone to your treasury; if the facing pit is empty nothing is taken.
  • A last stone in your treasury gives another turn.
  • The game ends when a side is empty; the player who emptied their side takes the stones left on the other.
  • Most stones in the treasury wins.

About the tradition

Mangala is the sowing game of Turkey and the lands of the former Ottoman world, played on boards of two rows of six or seven pits. It has long been a pastime of coffee houses and homes, and its pits are often called wells.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Deep Past and First Games (before about 3000 BCE), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Mesopotamia, Levant and Arabia.

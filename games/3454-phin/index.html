Phin

The phin is a small plucked lute of Laos and north-east Thailand. Three pairs of strings run over a neck with many wooden frets, and the body is carved from a single block with a flat foot. Its bright, quick tone suits lively folk tunes and songs.

How to play

  1. Three pairs of strings run up the neck, the lowest on the left. Each lane marks the fret where a note is played.
  2. A bead slides down the strings to its fret. Pluck the lane as the bead lands.
  3. Open lanes at the top sound the string without pressing.
  4. Try the market tune first, then call and reply, then the slow evening.
Rules at a glance
  • The notes come from a seven-note scale: D, E, F sharp, G, A, B and C, in two octaves.
  • Every phrase is played through twice.
  • A pluck on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra plucks cost points.
  • The market tune has two beats to the bar; the others have four.

About the tradition

The phin is played by singers and village bands in Laos and the neighbouring region. Makers cut the body from one piece of wood and set the frets in a pattern that matches the local scale.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Crossroads and Golden Ages (about 500 to 1500), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Southeast Asia and the Islands.

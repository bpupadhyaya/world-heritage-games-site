Pomaire pottery

Also known as Pomaire

Pomaire is a town near Santiago where potters make red clay cookware and ornaments. Casseroles, jugs and bowls are shaped on a wheel, dried and fired in a kiln, and many are left in the colour of the clay. Here you throw a wide casserole with two ears and slip each band.

How to play

  1. Look at the lit casserole in its window: it shows the shape and the slip on each band.
  2. Drag across the clay to set how wide each row is.
  3. Press Glaze, then tap a band and a slip: red clay, black or cream.
  4. Press Check to see your stars, or Watch and learn to see a round played for you.
Rules at a glance
  • Shape counts for 70 percent of the score: the closer each of the 12 rows is to the width of the lit target, the better.
  • Finish counts for 30 percent: a band scores when it carries the same swatch as the target.
  • The two ears are part of the picture; the shape counts the body of the casserole.
  • Undo takes back one stroke; Reshape goes back to the clay; Reset starts the shape again.
  • Three stars for 88 or more, two stars for 65 and one star for 40.

About the tradition

Pomaire lies in the central valley of Chile, a short way from Santiago. Its potters shape red clay into casseroles, pots and ornaments, and fire them in kilns; the ware is sold in the town to cooks from the whole region.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of The Americas: Andes and South America.

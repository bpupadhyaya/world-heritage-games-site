Bansuri bamboo flute

Also known as बाँसुरी · Nepali murali

The bansuri, called murali in Nepal, is a side-blown flute cut from a single length of bamboo with six finger holes. A player shapes the air with the lips and lifts fingers from the bottom upward to climb a scale, singing folk melodies and long phrases.

How to play

  1. Eight lanes stand for the notes of a scale, from the lowest note on the left to the octave on the right.
  2. Above each lane a diagram shows the six finger holes: filled holes are covered, open ones are lifted.
  3. Notes travel toward a line at the bottom; strike the lane just as a note arrives, and let long notes ring.
  4. Begin with the ladder up and down the scale, then play the hill-path tune and the call across the valley.
Rules at a glance
  • The scale is seven notes of a major mode plus the octave; pitches follow a fixed root.
  • Covering all six holes gives the lowest note; lifting fingers from the bottom upward raises it step by step.
  • A stroke within a hair of the note is perfect, a near one is good, and a missed one scores nothing.
  • An extra stroke while a song is running costs a few points.
  • Each song plays twice; three stars need roughly nine notes in ten on time.

About the tradition

Bamboo flutes are played in the hills and valleys of India and Nepal by herders, village singers and concert musicians alike. A flute is cut and tuned to a single key, so players keep a family of lengths.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Classical Worlds (about 1000 BCE to 500 CE), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of South Asia.

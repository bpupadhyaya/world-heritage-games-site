Lavash Baking

Also known as Լավաշ · tonir

In Armenia, lavash is a very thin flatbread. The baker stretches rested dough over a cushion, presses it on the hot clay wall of a sunken oven called a tonir, and lifts it out on a hook when it is blistered and golden.

How to play

  1. Stretch: drag over the dough on the cushion in steady passes to thin it evenly. Going too fast tears it.
  2. Stick: press To the oven, then press on the wall and hold; let go inside the lit part of the bar so the sheet clings.
  3. Bake: the wall is hotter low and on the fire side. Sheets blister and brown, and thin places colour first.
  4. Lift: tap a sheet to hook it off when it is golden. Pale is raw, dark is lost.
Rules at a glance
  • A sheet is scored on how thin and even it is, how cleanly it stuck, and its colour when lifted.
  • The round score is the mean of the sheets in the order.
  • A sheet that falls or burns scores nothing.

About the tradition

Lavash belongs to the everyday food culture of Armenia and its neighbours. Bakers, often women of a village working together, lean into the warm pit to press the dough on the wall and stack the sheets to keep for the week.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Persia, Caucasus and Central Asia.

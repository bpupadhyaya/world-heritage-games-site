Gambus

Also known as Malay lute of Brunei

The gambus is a lute of the Malay world. It has a deep, round pear-shaped body, a short fretless neck and a head that bends back, with several pairs of strings. Its warm voice carries sung stories and dance tunes, and its scales have wide steps found across the region.

How to play

  1. Four pairs of strings run down the short neck, the lowest on the left. The neck has no frets: each lane marks the finger place of a note.
  2. A bead slides down the strings to its place. Pluck the lane as the bead lands.
  3. Open lanes at the top sound the string without pressing.
  4. Try the desert wind first, then the quick courses, then the slow song.
Rules at a glance
  • The notes come from a scale with a wide step: D, E flat, F sharp, G, A, B flat, C, D and E.
  • Every phrase is played through twice.
  • A pluck on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra plucks cost points.
  • The quick courses figure has two beats to the bar.

About the tradition

The gambus travelled across the sea routes of the Malay world and is now played at weddings, in storytelling and in dance bands. Makers carve the pear body from a single block of wood and decorate the top by hand.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Crossroads and Golden Ages (about 500 to 1500), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Southeast Asia and the Islands.

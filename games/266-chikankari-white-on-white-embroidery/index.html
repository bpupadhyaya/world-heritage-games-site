Chikankari white-on-white embroidery

Also known as chikankari

Chikankari is white thread worked on fine pale cloth in and around Lucknow, in northern India. Flowers and leaves are stitched in raised back-stitch, while shadow work on the back of thin muslin shows through as soft milky shapes; small knots and drawn-thread lattices (jali) add texture.

How to play

  1. Look at the pattern on the card: each flower, leaf, knot or lattice has its own thread tone.
  2. Choose a thread tone, then a stitch from the tray.
  3. Touch the cloth to stitch it. The needle sweeps round the shape, and the same stitch appears at its mirror places.
  4. Press Check to compare your work with the pattern.
Rules at a glance
  • The cloth is mirrored left to right and top to bottom, so each stitch is made in up to four places at once.
  • A place counts only when both its stitch and its thread tone match the pattern.
  • Stitching the same stitch in the same tone again unpicks it.
  • Three stars need a near perfect copy; you can undo or clear as often as you like.

About the tradition

Chikankari is closely associated with Lucknow and nearby villages of Uttar Pradesh, where makers, mostly women, stitch on cloth that has first been block-printed with a washable design. It is recognised as a protected geographical craft of India.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin.

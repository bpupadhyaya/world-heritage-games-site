Omweso

Also known as Mweso

Four rows of eight pits and sixty-four seeds: each player owns the two rows nearest them. Seeds are sown in long relays round your own side, and a lucky ending takes both pits facing it.

How to play

  1. Choose any pit of yours that holds two or more seeds.
  2. Lift them and sow one by one, counter-clockwise, round your own two rows.
  3. If your last seed lands in a pit that was already occupied, lift that pit and keep sowing.
  4. When the last seed fills an occupied pit in your front row and both pits facing it are occupied, take those seeds and sow them on.
  5. The player who cannot find a pit with two seeds loses.
Rules at a glance
  • Sixty-four seeds; two seeds in every pit at the start.
  • A move starts from one of your pits holding at least two seeds.
  • Sowing runs counter-clockwise and stays on your own two rows.
  • A last seed in an occupied pit starts a new lap from that pit.
  • A last seed in an occupied front-row pit with both facing pits occupied takes the seeds of both pits.
  • Captured seeds are sown on from the pit where the capturing lap began.
  • A move ends when a lap finishes in an empty pit.
  • You lose when you have no pit with two or more seeds.

About the tradition

Omweso is the national sowing game of the Baganda in Uganda and was a pastime of the royal court. Players traditionally count the seeds as cattle; the game is still played in homes and at gatherings across the country.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Deep Past and First Games (before about 3000 BCE), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Africa: Great Lakes and Indian Ocean.

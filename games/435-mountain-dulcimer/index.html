Mountain dulcimer

Also known as Appalachian

A fretted zither played across the lap in the Appalachian hills. One melody string is stopped with a small wooden stick while drone strings hum beneath it, giving ballads and dance tunes their open, ringing sound.

How to play

  1. Look at the long fretboard: the melody string runs down the middle with a bar at every fret.
  2. Each lane is a fret on the melody string, and the last lane is the open drone string.
  3. Watch the beads travel along the string and strike the lane as each one lands on its fret.
  4. Learn the porch tune first, then try the reel and the waltz.
Rules at a glance
  • The frets give a seven-note scale with a flattened seventh, the traditional mountain mode.
  • The drone string is played open and always sounds the same low note.
  • A strum on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and stray strums cost points.
  • Each tune is played through twice.

About the tradition

Settlers in the Appalachian mountains made these instruments from local wood, and they became companions for ballads, hymn-like airs and dance tunes at home and on the porch. Makers and players across the region keep the tradition alive today.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Classical Worlds (about 1000 BCE to 500 CE), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of The Americas: North America.

Hichiriki

The hichiriki is a short double-reed pipe of Japan, a bamboo tube about as long as a hand with seven finger holes in front and two behind, bound in strips of bark. Its big flat reed gives a loud, reedy tone, and the player slides gently into each note.

How to play

  1. A covered hole glows; an open hole shows its dark bore. Six notes of the mode are lit down the tube.
  2. Each strike shows the fingering of that note.
  3. Let the long notes swell slowly before the next.
Rules at a glance
  • A phrase is a line of notes, each struck close to its beat.
  • Long notes swell slowly: strike on the beat and let them ring.
  • The round ends when the last note has been judged; the score is the share of the best possible.

About the tradition

In gagaku, the old court music of Japan, the hichiriki carries the main melody while other winds and strings move around it.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Classical Worlds (about 1000 BCE to 500 CE), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Japan and Korea.

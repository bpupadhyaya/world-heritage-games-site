Maple taffy on snow

Also known as tire d'erable · tire sur la neige

In the sugar-maple country of eastern Canada, hot maple syrup is poured in long ribbons onto clean packed snow. The cold sets it in moments into a soft, chewy taffy, which is wound up on a small wooden stick and eaten on the spot.

How to play

  1. Press and drag along the lit line to pour from the ladle. A steady pace lays a fat, even ribbon.
  2. Go too slowly and the syrup pools; go too fast and it breaks into drops. Refill when the ladle runs low.
  3. Press Set to let the cold work, then press the glowing dot at the end of each strip and drag along it to wind it onto its stick.
Rules at a glance
  • Syrup pours only while you press and drag; the pace of your hand sets the width of the ribbon.
  • A ladle holds enough for about two strips; an empty ladle pours only a thin thread.
  • Cold snow sets the syrup: hot it runs clear and glossy, then turns pale and chewy. A strip wound while still hot counts for less.
  • Score: how much of each lit line carries syrup (pooled or broken lines count less, and syrup poured off the lines is wasted) and how much of each strip you wind.

About the tradition

The treat belongs to the spring sugaring season of Quebec, Ontario and the Atlantic provinces of Canada and neighbouring New England, when sap from sugar maples is boiled down to syrup in sugar shacks. Families and sugar-bush farms keep the custom alive each late winter.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of The Americas: North America.

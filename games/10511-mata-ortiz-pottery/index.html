Mata Ortiz pottery

Also known as cerámica de Mata Ortiz

In the village of Mata Ortiz in Chihuahua, potters revived the painted ware of the old Casas Grandes culture. Jars are built up by hand with thin walls, polished and painted with fine geometric designs in natural pigments, then fired in open fires. Here you shape a very round jar and choose the colour of each band.

How to play

  1. Look at the lit jar in its tin niche: it shows the shape and the colour of each band.
  2. Drag across the clay to set how wide each row is.
  3. Press Glaze, then tap a band and a colour: cream slip, black paint or rust red.
  4. Press Check to see your stars, or Watch and learn to see a round played for you.
Rules at a glance
  • Shape counts for 70 percent of the score: the closer each of the 12 rows is to the width of the lit target, the better.
  • Finish counts for 30 percent: a band scores when it carries the same swatch as the target.
  • The sawtooth designs are part of the picture; the colour of the band decides what is painted on it.
  • Undo takes back one stroke; Reshape goes back to the clay; Reset starts the shape again.
  • Three stars for 88 or more, two stars for 65 and one star for 40.

About the tradition

Mata Ortiz is a small village in Chihuahua, northern Mexico. Its potters took inspiration from the old painted pottery of the nearby Casas Grandes culture and rebuilt the craft: coiled walls, a polished surface, and fine geometric painting fired in open fires.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of The Americas: Mesoamerica.

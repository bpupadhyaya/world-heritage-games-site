Palm sugar boiling and mould-filling

Also known as ស្ករត្នោត

Sap from the sugar palm is boiled down in a wide wok over a wood fire, stirred steadily until it turns to golden caramel, then ladled into small moulds to set into cakes of palm sugar.

How to play

  1. Sweep the paddle left and right to each lit mark on the wok, just as the ring closes on it.
  2. Press Feed and Ease to keep the fire inside the green window.
  3. Press Test to see how the syrup falls from the spoon, and press Ladle when it reaches the green line on the card.
  4. Press and hold on a mould and let go at its line. Fill every mould before the syrup sets.
Rules at a glance
  • Steady stirring on the beat keeps the sugar from catching on the bottom of the wok.
  • A fire that is too strong froths the thin sap over the edge; one that is too weak stalls the boil.
  • The syrup grows stiffer the longer it stands, and in the end it sets in the wok.
  • Ladle only when the syrup reaches the green line on the test card.
  • Each mould should be filled level with its line; overfilling spills syrup and counts against you.
  • Your score is made of the stirring, the fire, the test and how close each mould is to its line.

About the tradition

In Cambodia, sugar is made from the sweet sap of the sugar palm, which climbers collect each day in bamboo tubes during the dry season, notably in Kampong Speu province. The sap is simmered for hours in a wide wok over a wood fire and stirred until it thickens and darkens to caramel. The hot syrup is then poured into small moulds and left to set into cakes.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Southeast Asia and the Islands.

Valiha tube zither

Also known as Valiha

The valiha is a tube zither from the highlands of Madagascar: a length of bamboo with strings running its whole length, each lifted on a pair of small bridges and tuned to a note of the scale. Played with both hands, it gives rolling, interlocking melodies that ring on like running water.

How to play

  1. Twelve strings run along the tube, low notes on the left and high notes on the right, each named by its pitch.
  2. A bead glides down a string toward the plucking place. Pluck that string just as the bead reaches it.
  3. Begin with the rolling pattern counted in two groups of three, then try the weaving tune that passes notes between the hands.
  4. Watch and learn plays a falling cascade for you to hear and follow before you try it yourself.
Rules at a glance
  • Twelve strings are tuned to a seven-note major scale, running a little over an octave and a half.
  • The first and third songs are counted in six pulses, grouped three and three; the second is counted in four.
  • A stroke on the pulse is perfect, a near one is good, and an extra stroke costs points.
  • Each song is played twice through.

About the tradition

The valiha is a symbol of Madagascar's highlands and has been played there for many generations. Makers once cut the strings from the bamboo's own skin; today players often use fine wire, and the instrument is kept alive by family teaching and festival music.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Classical Worlds (about 1000 BCE to 500 CE), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Africa: Great Lakes and Indian Ocean.

Barro negro pottery

Also known as barro negro

In the Oaxaca valley, potters raise jars and pitchers from grey clay, rub the leather-hard surface with a smooth quartz stone until it shines, and fire it in a smothered kiln that turns the clay black. Some pieces are cut with fine pierced patterns before firing. Here you shape a jar and choose the finish of each band.

How to play

  1. Look at the lit jar on its shelf: it shows the shape and the finish of each band.
  2. Drag across the turning clay to set how wide each row is.
  3. Press Glaze, then tap a band and a finish: burnished black, matt grey-black or raw clay.
  4. Press Check to see your stars, or Watch and learn to see a round played for you.
Rules at a glance
  • Shape counts for 70 percent of the score: the closer each of the 12 rows is to the width of the lit target, the better.
  • Finish counts for 30 percent: a band scores when it carries the same swatch as the target.
  • A pierced band, a polished band and a matt band are different finishes in the picture; the three finishes are the three swatches.
  • Undo takes back one stroke; Reshape goes back to the clay; Reset starts the shape again.
  • Three stars for 88 or more, two stars for 65 and one star for 40.

About the tradition

Black clay pottery is made in the Oaxaca valley of southern Mexico, most of all in San Bartolo Coyotepec. A piece is shaped by hand, polished with a stone while the clay is leather-hard, and fired in a smothered kiln that darkens the clay and keeps the shine.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of The Americas: Mesoamerica.

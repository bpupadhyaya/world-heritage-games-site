Nubian house painting

Also known as Nuba · Nubian wall decoration

Along the Nile in southern Egypt, mud-brick houses are whitewashed and then painted with bands of geometry in turquoise, ochre, red-brown and black. Triangles, zigzags, chequers and arched doorways are brushed on with a frayed palm-fibre brush.

How to play

  1. Look at the wall plan on the card: each band and shape has its own colour.
  2. Choose a colour, then a shape from the tray.
  3. Touch the wall to paint. The same shape is painted half a turn round the wall at the same moment.
  4. Press Check to compare your wall with the plan.
Rules at a glance
  • The wall is the same when turned half a circle, so each press is painted in two places at once.
  • A place counts only when both its shape and its colour match the plan.
  • Painting the same shape in the same colour again wipes it off.
  • Three stars need a near perfect copy; you can undo or clear as often as you like.

About the tradition

Nubian houses of the Nile valley are built of mud brick and often finished with a coat of whitewash that makers then decorate with painted friezes, door surrounds and borders. The designs are original to each household and are handed down within families.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Africa: Nile and the Horn.

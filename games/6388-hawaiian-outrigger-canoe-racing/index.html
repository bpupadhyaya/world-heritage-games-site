Hawaiian outrigger canoe racing

Also known as wa'a racing

Six paddlers, one long hull and a float held out on lashed booms: the sprint across open water is won by strokes that fall together.

How to play

  1. Watch the ring close on the pad and strike the moment it meets the pad.
  2. Alternate sides with every beat: two strokes on one side drift you off line.
  3. Keep the stroke steady through the chop and past the marker buoy, and cross the line first.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat gives a full push; off-beat strokes spend strength and give almost nothing.
  • Rough water shrinks the timing window.
  • Stars: first with a steady rhythm for three; first alone for two; on the podium for one.

About the tradition

Outrigger canoes are paddled in teams across the Pacific, where the float on its booms keeps a slender hull steady in open swell. Crews practise for seasons to move as one body.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Age of Contact and Craft (about 1500 to 1800), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Pacific and Oceania.

Federkielstickerei

Also known as Quill embroidery

In the Alpine regions of Austria and Bavaria, peacock quills are split into fine strips and sewn onto dark leather with thread to make flowers, leaves and tendrils. The glossy strips catch the light and are worked onto belts, braces and trousers.

How to play

  1. Pick a quill colour from the bundle and a shape from the tray.
  2. Touch a square on the leather to sew that shape there; it is mirrored across and down.
  3. Copy the border shown on the chart, then press Check.
Rules at a glance
  • The border is mirrored left to right and top to bottom, so every shape you sew is also sewn on its partner squares.
  • A square counts only when its shape and its quill colour both match the border you are copying.
  • Three stars need a nearly perfect copy; you can undo or clear as often as you like.

About the tradition

Quill embroidery is a craft of the Alpine regions of Austria and Bavaria, worked mostly on leather belts, braces and the trousers of regional costume. Each strip is cut from a quill by hand and sewn down one piece at a time, which is why a single belt takes many hours.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Northern and Eastern Europe.

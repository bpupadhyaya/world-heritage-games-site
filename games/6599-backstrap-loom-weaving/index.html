Backstrap loom weaving

On a backstrap loom the warp is stretched between a fixed post and a strap that goes around the weaver's lower back, so leaning back or forward changes the tension. A flat sword packs each weft and a pick-up stick lifts the pattern threads, letting coloured floats stand out on a narrow belt or band.

How to play

  1. Pick a thread colour from the skeins and a pattern shape from the tray.
  2. Touch a square on the belt to pick up that shape there; it is mirrored left to right.
  3. Copy the belt shown on the card, then press Check.
Rules at a glance
  • The belt is mirrored left to right, so each shape is picked up on up to two squares.
  • A square counts only when its shape and its thread colour both match the belt you are copying.
  • Three stars need a nearly perfect copy; you can undo or clear as often as you like.

About the tradition

Backstrap looms are among the simplest and most widespread looms, found in the Americas from Mesoamerica to the Andes and across Southeast and East Asia. The whole loom can be rolled up and carried, which suits narrow belts, bands and cloth woven in many homes.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin.

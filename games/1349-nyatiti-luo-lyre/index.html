Nyatiti Luo lyre

Also known as Nyatiti

The nyatiti is the eight-string lyre of the Luo people of Kenya. A long wooden trough is covered with hide and pinned at the edges, short carved arms rise from it, and the strings are tied to a beaded crossbar. The player sits and plucks with both hands while a ring of small iron bells on the toe keeps the beat.

How to play

  1. Eight strings run from the beaded crossbar to the bridge, the lowest on the left. Each lane marks a place to pluck a string.
  2. Beads slide down the strings. Pluck the lane as the bead lands.
  3. The round lane at the foot of the lyre is the toe rattle: tap it on the beat, as the player taps the bells with the foot.
  4. Learn the rattle pattern first, then add the strings over it.
Rules at a glance
  • The strings are tuned to an even seven-note scale, with the eighth string an octave above the first.
  • The patterns are played through twice.
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Patterns count eight pulses, split into 3 + 3 + 2.

About the tradition

The nyatiti is the main instrument of Luo song in western Kenya, played to accompany praise and story. The player keeps a steady rattle with the toe while the hands answer one another on the strings.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Classical Worlds (about 1000 BCE to 500 CE), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Africa: Great Lakes and Indian Ocean.

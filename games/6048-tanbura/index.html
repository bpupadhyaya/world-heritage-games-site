Tanbura

Also known as Tanboura · Nubian Lyre · Lyre

The tanbura is a six-string lyre of the Red Sea coast and Nubia. A wide, shallow bowl is covered with hide and studded at the rim; two short, thick arms are set with cowrie shells and carry a square crossbar lashed with rope. It is plucked and strummed to carry dance songs.

How to play

  1. Six strings run from the rope-lashed crossbar to the bridge, the lowest on the left. Each lane marks a place to pluck one string.
  2. Beads slide down the strings. Pluck the lane as the bead lands.
  3. The songs repeat a short figure over and over, as in dance music.
  4. Start slowly with the first figure, then speed up as it settles.
Rules at a glance
  • The six strings are tuned to a scale with a lowered seventh.
  • Every figure is played through twice.
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Figures count four beats to the bar, with a lift on the last.

About the tradition

In the ports and villages of the Red Sea coast the tanbura accompanies songs for gatherings and work. Its wide bowl gives a low, rounded note, and the cowrie shells on the arms are an old mark of the coast.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Classical Worlds (about 1000 BCE to 500 CE), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Africa: Nile and the Horn.

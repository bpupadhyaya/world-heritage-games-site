Guacharaca

The guacharaca is a scraped idiophone made of a grooved palm-wood tube scraped with a three-pronged fork, used in vallenato. The player rubs the fork along the notches to make a dry, scratchy rhythm.

How to play

  1. Four places are lit along the tube: Down and Up scrapes, Scratch in the middle, Knock at the lower end.
  2. The fork rides the grooves as each stroke sounds; the grooves it crosses gleam.
  3. A ring closes on the place to strike. Keep the scrape dry and steady.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Each pattern repeats twice.
  • The round ends when the last stroke has been judged; the score is the share of the best possible.

About the tradition

In vallenato from the Caribbean coast of Colombia it sits with the box drum and the accordion, giving the music a dry, scratchy edge.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of The Americas: Andes and South America.

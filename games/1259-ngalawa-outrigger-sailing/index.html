Ngalawa outrigger sailing

Also known as Ngalawa outrigger sailing (Comoros) · Comoros

A narrow outrigger with a crab-claw sail: balance it in the swell and keep the cloth full as the monsoon wind swings.

How to play

  1. Hold the left or the right side to ease or haul the sail.
  2. Keep the white dot on the gold marker: it follows the wind as it shifts.
  3. Stay on the marker as you round the buoys.
Rules at a glance
  • Speed comes from how well the sail matches the wind.
  • The current gives every boat a small push.
  • Stars: first with a steady trim for three; first alone for two; on the podium for one.

About the tradition

Outrigger canoes with triangular sails on curved spars fish and carry goods around the Comoros and the Swahili coast. The float on its booms lets a slender hull carry a big sail.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Age of Contact and Craft (about 1500 to 1800), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Africa: Great Lakes and Indian Ocean.

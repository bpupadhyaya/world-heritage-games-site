Timbales

Timbales are a pair of shallow metal-shelled drums on a stand, with a cowbell mounted between them. Played with thin sticks on the heads and on the shells, they drive the dance bands of Cuba with sharp, ringing patterns.

How to play

  1. Two drums stand side by side, the low one on the left and the high one on the right, with a cowbell above and between them.
  2. A ring closes on the place to strike. Strike as it lands.
  3. The steel shell of the high drum, the paila, gives a ringing click, and the cowbell marks the bar.
  4. Learn the shell pattern first, then add the drums.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Each pattern repeats twice.
  • The patterns count eight pulses, split 3 + 3 + 2.

About the tradition

In Cuban dance music the timbalero plays a steady pattern on the shell, switches to the cowbell for the loud sections, and fills between with the drums. The drums were adapted from the orchestral kettle drums.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of The Americas: Caribbean.

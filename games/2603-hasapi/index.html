Hasapi

The hasapi is a two-string lute of the Batak people of Sumatra. Its body is carved from one piece of wood in the shape of a small boat, and a few raised frets mark the notes of the song scale. Players pluck quick patterns that answer each other across the two strings.

How to play

  1. Two strings run up the neck, the lower on the left. Each lane marks the fret where a note is played.
  2. A bead slides down the string to its fret. Pluck the lane as the bead lands.
  3. Notes on the high string sit further up the neck.
  4. Try the river song first, then the twin strings, then the slow greeting.
Rules at a glance
  • The notes come from a five-note scale: D, E, F sharp, A and B, in two octaves.
  • Every phrase is played through twice.
  • A pluck on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra plucks cost points.
  • The twin strings figure has two beats to the bar.

About the tradition

Boat lutes like the hasapi have been played in the Sumatran highlands for generations, often in small ensembles. Makers carve the body and decorate it with the red, black and white scrolls of the region.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Crossroads and Golden Ages (about 500 to 1500), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Southeast Asia and the Islands.

Galician gaita

The gaita is the bagpipe of Galicia in north-west Spain. A round bag, covered in bright cloth and braid, feeds a bright conical chanter, while a very long drone rises over the piper's shoulder. It is usually played with a drum.

How to play

  1. A covered hole glows; an open hole shows its dark bore. The chanter is the pipe with the finger holes.
  2. The bag gives each time a note sounds, and the drones keep the home note.
  3. Strike close to the beat; a repeated note is cut with a quick grace note.
Rules at a glance
  • Seven notes of the chanter, each struck close to its beat.
  • The drone sounds from the first breath to the last: the notes sit on top of it.
  • The round ends when the last note has been judged; the score is the share of the best possible.

About the tradition

Its bright, strong voice leads the dances and street music of Galicia.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Classical Worlds (about 1000 BCE to 500 CE), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Mediterranean.

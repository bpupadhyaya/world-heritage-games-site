Lepapata kudu horn

Also known as Lepatata

The lepapata is a horn made from a greater kudu or sable antelope, used for celebrations and warning calls. The twisted horn is blown through a mouth hole to sound a loud signal, and the player can reach a few notes of the overtone series by tightening the lips.

How to play

  1. Four notes of the horn are lit along it, the higher ones nearer the mouth hole.
  2. A band of breath runs from the mouth to the note you strike, and rings of sound leave the opening.
  3. Long notes swell slowly: strike close to the beat and let them ring.
Rules at a glance
  • A call is a line of long notes, each struck close to its beat.
  • Long notes swell slowly: strike on the beat and let them ring.
  • The round ends when the last note has been judged; the score is the share of the best possible.

About the tradition

It carries far over open country, which is why it is used for signals as well as for music.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Crossroads and Golden Ages (about 500 to 1500), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Africa: Southern Africa.

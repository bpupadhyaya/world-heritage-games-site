Bell chime

A handbell choir plays a tune on a set of tuned brass bells, one bell for each note. The bells stand on a padded table in two ranks, like the white and black keys of a piano, and each one is rung by a quick swing of the wrist.

How to play

  1. Ten bells are lit: seven plain notes in front and three sharps behind.
  2. Ring the bell a closing ring marks. It rocks and its clapper rings the brass.
  3. Larger bells are lower. Let a ring die away before the same bell is needed again.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Each pattern repeats twice.
  • The round ends when the last stroke has been judged; the score is the share of the best possible.

About the tradition

Each ringer in a choir looks after one or two bells and plays only those notes, so a tune only appears when everyone rings in time.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin.

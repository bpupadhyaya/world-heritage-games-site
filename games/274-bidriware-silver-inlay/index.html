Bidriware silver inlay

Also known as ಬಿದರಿ

Bidriware is the dark, silver-patterned metalwork of Bidar in southern India. A cast plate of zinc alloy is engraved with fine grooves, silver wire is hammered into them, and the whole piece is blackened so that only the silver shines.

How to play

  1. The framed plate shows the finished pattern you are making.
  2. Engrave the lit lines with the graver so every groove lies on a line.
  3. Press the silver wire along the grooves you cut; wire only settles where a groove exists.
  4. Strike along the wire with the hammer so it sits flat, then blacken the plate and see the silver stand out.
Rules at a glance
  • The work has three steps: engraving, laying the wire, and peening it flat with the hammer; the final score is the mean of the three.
  • Engraving is judged by how closely each groove follows its line.
  • Wire is pressed in only along grooves that have been engraved, so a missed groove means a gap in the silver.
  • Every stretch of wire should be struck with the hammer; unstruck wire counts against you.
  • Nothing is scored for doing nothing, and the last stroke can be taken back during engraving.

About the tradition

Bidriware takes its name from Bidar, where metalworkers have made it since the days of the Bahmani sultanate, drawing on Persian-influenced floral designs. Makers there cast a zinc and copper alloy, inlay it with silver, and darken it with a special soil from the old fort; the craft is now protected as a regional speciality of Karnataka.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin.

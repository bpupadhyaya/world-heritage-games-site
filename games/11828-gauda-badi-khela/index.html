Gauda Badi Khela

Also known as ଗଉଡ଼ ବାଡ଼ି ଖେଳ · Badi Khela · Laudi Khela · Danda Khela

An Odisha stick dance of the Gopal cattle-herding communities, performed in groups in the spring season, with short sticks struck together in rhythm.

How to play

  1. Four hands rise from below, each holding a long cane. Four places are lit: the left pair, the middle pair, the right pair, and all four together.
  2. Strike the place a closing ring marks, on the beat, so those canes meet in the air.
  3. Keep a steady count: the pattern repeats as the game goes round.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and an extra stroke costs points.
  • Each pattern is played twice.
  • The round ends when the last stroke has been judged; the score is the share of the best possible.

About the tradition

Twelve to twenty-four men in matching dress dance in teams under captains, each holding two short sticks and striking the sticks of their neighbours in rhythm to a song, with friendly contests between groups.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of South Asia.

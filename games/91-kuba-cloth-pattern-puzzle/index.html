Kuba cloth pattern puzzle

Also known as Kuba raffia cloth

Kuba cloth is made from the fibre of the raffia palm in the Kasai region of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Men weave the plain cloth and women decorate it with stitched lines, cut-pile embroidery and sewn-on patches, in bold geometric designs whose lines break off and start again just out of step.

How to play

  1. Pick a thread colour and a shape from the tray under the cloth.
  2. Touch a square to place that shape: it also appears on the opposite square, as if the cloth were turned half way round.
  3. Copy the small cloth shown above, then press Check.
Rules at a glance
  • The cloth is turned half way round on itself, so each shape you place is copied to the square opposite it.
  • Shapes turn with the half turn, so a fret that runs one way answers with one running the other way.
  • A square counts only when its shape and colour both match the target; three stars need a nearly perfect copy.

About the tradition

Kuba cloth comes from the Kuba kingdom region of the Kasai in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Cloths are often made in groups, with several women stitching the blocks of one large skirt, so no two are alike.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Africa: Sahel, West and Central.

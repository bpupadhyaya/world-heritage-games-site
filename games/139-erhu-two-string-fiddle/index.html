Erhu Two-String Fiddle

Also known as Erhu 二胡

The erhu is a Chinese fiddle with just two strings and no fingerboard. A six-sided wooden barrel, faced with snakeskin, rests on the player's thigh while a horsehair bow passes between the strings. Notes are made by touching the strings in the air and sliding, so the sound can sigh, sob or gallop.

How to play

  1. Two strings climb the long neck; each coloured lane marks the finger place of one note, the lower notes on the inner string and the higher ones on the outer.
  2. A bead slides down toward its finger place. Bow that note just as the bead lands.
  3. Long notes show a tail: let them sing for their full length, as a bow would.
  4. Start with the slow sighing line, then try the galloping figure and the rising-and-falling tune.
Rules at a glance
  • The strings are tuned a fifth apart, here D and A, and the notes follow the D major scale.
  • A bow stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Long notes are judged at their start only.
  • Every tune is played through twice.

About the tradition

The erhu belongs to the large family of Chinese bowed fiddles and has been played in village ensembles, street music and theatre for many centuries. Today it is heard as a solo voice and in orchestras, loved for glides that sound close to the human voice.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Crossroads and Golden Ages (about 500 to 1500), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of China.

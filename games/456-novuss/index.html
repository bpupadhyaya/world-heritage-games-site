Novuss

Also known as Koroona

A square wooden board with a pocket in each corner. A slender cue drives a heavy puck into black and light discs, and the aim is to send the discs into the corners while keeping the puck itself out of them. A national pastime of Latvia and Estonia.

How to play

  1. Press on the near edge where you want to start, then pull the cue back along the line you want.
  2. Release to strike. The dotted line shows the puck's path and where it bounces.
  3. Drive discs into the corner pockets: dark ones are worth two, light ones one.
  4. Eight strokes make a game. Beat your best total.
Rules at a glance
  • The board is square with raised wooden edges and a pocket in each corner.
  • Every stroke starts from the near edge; you choose the spot along it.
  • The puck and the discs bounce off the edges and off each other, and slow down on the polished wood.
  • A dark disc in a pocket scores 2 and a light disc scores 1.
  • If the puck itself drops into a pocket you lose 1 point.
  • A game is eight strokes, and it ends early if every disc is pocketed.
  • Stars are awarded for total score.

About the tradition

Novuss is thought to have reached the Baltic coast in the 1920s and became a national game in Latvia and Estonia, where it is played in clubs, homes and schools. The Latvian federation was founded in 1963, and Baltic communities play it abroad.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Crossroads and Golden Ages (about 500 to 1500), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Northern and Eastern Europe.

Kalamatianos

Also known as καλαματιανός

A Greek circle and chain dance in a seven-beat rhythm, led by the dancer at the front who waves a handkerchief while the line follows in joined hands.

How to play

  1. Four places are lit beside the line: Step, Cross, Lift and the leader's Turn.
  2. A ring closes on a place when that movement is due. Tap the place as the ring closes and the whole line answers.
  3. The rhythm has seven beats to the bar: count it three, two, two.
Rules at a glance
  • A tap right on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and taps with no ring open cost points.
  • Each pattern is played twice through; every pattern is counted in sevens.
  • Patterns are simplified teaching figures in the style of the dance, not choreography to copy.

About the tradition

The Kalamatianos is danced across Greece at weddings and village celebrations. Dancers hold hands at shoulder height in an open circle or a line that curves around the square; the leader at the head of the line sets the steps and often holds a handkerchief, which is waved and turned as the line follows.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Mediterranean.

Yunluo

The yunluo is a frame of ten small flat gongs, each tuned to its own note, hung in three rows. Held or set on a stand, it is struck with a small mallet and gives a bright, silvery tune.

How to play

  1. Ten gongs are lit in three rows: the low notes at the bottom, the high ones at the top.
  2. Strike the gong a closing ring marks. It swings and shimmers.
  3. Moving up the frame climbs in pitch.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Each pattern repeats twice.
  • The round ends when the last stroke has been judged; the score is the share of the best possible.

About the tradition

The gongs are small enough to hang close together, so a single player can reach every note. Its bright tone is often heard in folk ensembles and opera bands.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Classical Worlds (about 1000 BCE to 500 CE), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of China.

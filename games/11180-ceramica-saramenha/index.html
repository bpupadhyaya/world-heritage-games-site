Cerâmica saramenha

In towns of Minas Gerais in Brazil, such as Ouro Branco, potters follow a Portuguese way of making hand-thrown earthenware with a warm glaze. A tall water jar with two ears and a narrow neck is a typical piece. Here you throw such a jar and glaze each band in honey, brown or green.

How to play

  1. Look at the lit jar in its blue window frame: it shows the shape and the glaze of each band.
  2. Drag across the clay to set how wide each row is.
  3. Press Glaze, then tap a band and a glaze: honey, brown or green.
  4. Press Check to see your stars, or Watch and learn to see a round played for you.
Rules at a glance
  • Shape counts for 70 percent of the score: the closer each of the 12 rows is to the width of the lit target, the better.
  • Finish counts for 30 percent: a band scores when it carries the same swatch as the target.
  • The two ears are part of the picture; the shape counts the body of the jar.
  • Undo takes back one stroke; Reshape goes back to the clay; Reset starts the shape again.
  • Three stars for 88 or more, two stars for 65 and one star for 40.

About the tradition

Saramenha pottery came to Minas Gerais from Portugal and is still made in some of its towns. The ware is thrown by hand, glazed in warm browns and greens, and fired in kilns.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of The Americas: Andes and South America.

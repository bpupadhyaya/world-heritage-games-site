Jebena coffee brewing

Also known as Buna · jebena

In Ethiopia and Eritrea coffee is made fresh from green beans: roasted in a flat pan over charcoal, pounded in a wooden mortar, boiled in a round-bellied clay pot and poured from a height into a row of small cups.

How to play

  1. Roast: drag round and round in the pan to turn the beans, then pull the pan when the colour reaches the green band.
  2. Pound: tap as the ring closes on the mouth of the mortar.
  3. Boil: fan the charcoal or set the pot aside to keep the brew in the green band of the neck window without overflowing.
  4. Pour: press and hold, sweep slowly along the cups with the spout held high, and bring every cup to the same level.
Rules at a glance
  • Each step is scored; press the lit button to finish a step.
  • Beans left on the hot place of the pan go dark first.
  • Taps off the beat make the beans jump out of the mortar.
  • Never let the brew rise past the red mark in the neck.
  • A shaky pour, or the last grounds from the pot, cloud the cups.

About the tradition

Across Ethiopia and Eritrea, coffee is a daily household craft. Villages often favour their own shape of jebena, a clay pot with a narrow neck that holds back the grounds as the coffee is poured. The skill lies in judging colour, sound and aroma at every step.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Africa: Nile and the Horn.

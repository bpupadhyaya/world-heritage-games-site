Magimagi

Magimagi is cord plaited from coconut-husk fibre, flat in rows of chevrons, then lashed to frames and mats. Black, red-brown and cream cords draw the bands. Copy the pattern, which mirrors both ways.

How to play

  1. Pick a cord colour and a pattern shape.
  2. Plait it in; the mirrored places are plaited too.
  3. Check the cordwork against the pattern.
  4. Watch and learn shows a finished round, one placement at a time.
Rules at a glance
  • The cordwork mirrors left to right and top to bottom.
  • Cord colours must match.
  • Placing the same shape in the same colour again, or choosing the eraser, clears it and its mirror partners.
  • The result compares your piece with the pattern cell by cell: three stars at 98 percent or more, two at 85, one at 60.
  • The pattern to copy is always shown beside the work and as a faint guide on the cloth.

About the tradition

Magimagi is the coconut-fibre cord of Fiji, plaited by hand and used to lash house frames, canoes and mats. Its black and red-brown chevron bands are a familiar sight in Fijian building.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Pacific and Oceania.

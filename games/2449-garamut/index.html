Garamut

The garamut is a huge slit drum of Papua New Guinea: a log hollowed through a long narrow slit, its ends carved and painted. The two lips of the slit are cut to different thicknesses, so one lip booms and the other rings, and the sound carries across a valley or over water.

How to play

  1. The log lies on two forked posts: the thick near lip of the slit below, the thin far lip above, the carved end to the left.
  2. Low is the thick lip, High the thin lip, End the carved cap and Body the belly of the log.
  3. A ring closes on the place to strike. Strike as it lands.
  4. Begin with slow strokes on the low lip, then answer on the high lip.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Each pattern repeats twice.
  • The patterns count four beats to the bar.

About the tradition

Villages along rivers and coasts keep large slit drums in a shared house or clearing. Different patterns on the two lips carry news and calls from one village to the next, so a player is also a messenger.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Pacific and Oceania.

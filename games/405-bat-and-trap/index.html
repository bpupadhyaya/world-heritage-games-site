Bat and Trap

A pub-garden game of south-east England: a rubber ball rests on a hinged wooden trap, is flipped into the air by a knock of the bat, then struck out across the lawn between two tall posts. The farther it lands inside the field, the more it scores.

How to play

  1. Press at the trap, pull back and let go: the bat strikes the ball and sends it up the lawn.
  2. Aim between the two posts; a ball that drifts wide is out of play.
  3. Land the ball in the far strips of grass for the biggest scores. Six strikes make a round, and there are two rounds.
Rules at a glance
  • The ball must travel between the two posts; a ball that goes wide counts nothing.
  • The lawn is marked in strips; the farther the strip the ball lands in, the more it is worth (one to four).
  • A ball that carries past the end of the lawn is lost.
  • A light breeze can push the ball sideways, so allow for it.
  • Each round is six strikes and a game is two rounds; your total decides the stars.

About the tradition

A pub game of Kent and the south coast of England, played on garden lawns and in league matches for generations, with a wooden trap, a solid rubber ball and two tall posts on a pitch measured in yards.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Modern Heritage (1800 to today), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Celtic and Atlantic Europe.

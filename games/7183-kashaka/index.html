Kashaka

The kashaka is a pair of small bean-filled gourds joined by a string. One gourd is held while the other is spun round the hand on its cord, so the beans rattle in a long whirring roll that stops with a catch.

How to play

  1. Three places are lit: the Held gourd, the orbit of the Spinning gourd, and the Catch where the cord is stopped.
  2. The spinning gourd runs faster as you keep the roll going. A ring closes on the place to strike.
  3. End each bar with the catch.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Each pattern repeats twice.
  • The round ends when the last stroke has been judged; the score is the share of the best possible.

About the tradition

The two gourds make a rattle that can be shaken, swung and spun, so one player can sound both a short beat and a long roll.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Africa: Sahel, West and Central.

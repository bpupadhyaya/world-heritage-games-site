Assisi embroidery

Assisi embroidery is a voided cross-stitch from the Umbrian hill town of Assisi. The birds, beasts and flowers are left as bare linen and outlined in a dark double-running stitch, while the ground around them is filled with tiny cross-stitches, usually in a single colour.

How to play

  1. Pick an outline thread from the cards and a motif from the tray.
  2. Touch a square on the linen: the crosses step back, the shape is outlined in your thread, and its mirror partners follow.
  3. Copy the chart shown on the card, then press Check.
Rules at a glance
  • The chart is mirrored left to right and top to bottom, so every shape you work is also worked on its partner squares.
  • A square counts only when its shape and its outline colour both match the chart you are copying.
  • Three stars need a nearly perfect copy; you can undo or clear as often as you like.

About the tradition

This counted work belongs to Assisi in Umbria, in central Italy, where medieval textile motifs of birds, animals and plants are stitched as bare shapes against a filled ground. It was revived as a local craft in the early twentieth century and is still taught and sold in the town.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Mediterranean.

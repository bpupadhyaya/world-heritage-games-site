La Chamba pottery

Also known as La Chamba · Chamba black pottery

In La Chamba, in the Tolima region of Colombia, potters build cooking pots by hand from red clay, polish them with a stone and fire them in pits so that the walls turn black. A pot with a domed lid and two small handles is the classic form. Here you shape a squat pot and choose the colour of each band.

How to play

  1. Look at the lit pot in its ring of stones: it shows the walls and the colour of each band.
  2. Drag across the clay to set how wide each row is; the lid and handles are already in place.
  3. Press Glaze, then tap a band and a colour: smoke black, red ochre or polished brown.
  4. Press Check to see your stars, or Watch and learn to see a round played for you.
Rules at a glance
  • Shape counts for 70 percent of the score: the closer each of the 12 rows is to the width of the lit target, the better.
  • Finish counts for 30 percent: a band scores when it carries the same swatch as the target.
  • The lid and the two handles are part of the picture; the shape counts the body of the pot.
  • Undo takes back one stroke; Reshape goes back to the clay; Reset starts the shape again.
  • Three stars for 88 or more, two stars for 65 and one star for 40.

About the tradition

La Chamba is a village on the Magdalena river in Tolima, Colombia, known for polished cooking ware made without a wheel. The red clay is formed by hand, burnished with a stone, and fired in a pit, which leaves the walls black.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of The Americas: Andes and South America.

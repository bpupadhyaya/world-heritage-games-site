Litungu

The litungu is the lyre of the Luhya people of Kenya. A deep round bowl of pale wood is covered with a skin that has one sound hole, and two tall arms sweep out and curl in toward a slim crossbar. Eight strings are tied on with dark cuffs. The wood is burnt with black patterns.

How to play

  1. Eight strings run from the cuffs on the crossbar down to the bridge, the lowest on the left. Each lane marks a place to pluck a string.
  2. A bead slides down a string. Pluck the lane as it lands.
  3. The songs rise and fall through the eight notes in a steady swing.
  4. Learn the opening phrase by echo, then play it through.
Rules at a glance
  • The eight strings are tuned to five notes spread over an octave and more.
  • Songs are played through twice.
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Songs count four beats to the bar.

About the tradition

The litungu is played in western Kenya to carry story and song. Makers burn bold patterns into the pale wood of the arms and bowl, and the long curling arms are its most recognizable feature.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Classical Worlds (about 1000 BCE to 500 CE), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Africa: Great Lakes and Indian Ocean.

Stickball

Also known as Street Stickball · Stoopball

A sawn-off broom handle, a pink rubber ball and a city street: one player flips the ball up and swings, and the hit is measured in sewers, the manhole covers that run down the road. It grew up in the crowded blocks of New York and other big cities of the north-eastern United States, played by children after school and by grown-ups on summer evenings.

How to play

  1. Press at the batter and pull back to set the line and the swing's strength.
  2. Release to swing: the dotted line shows where the ball will fly.
  3. Six swings make a round. The further down the street the ball lands, the more it is worth.
Rules at a glance
  • A ball landing past the first manhole scores 1, a single; past the second 2, a double; past the third 3, a triple.
  • A ball that carries to the far end of the street is a home run and scores 4.
  • A swing that falls short of the first manhole scores nothing.
  • Parked cars and the kerbs bounce the ball but never stop it from scoring.
  • All six swings count when the round ends.

About the tradition

Street stickball was played on the paved blocks of New York, Philadelphia and Boston through the twentieth century, using a broom handle and a rubber ball. Players used what the street offered: manhole covers as bases or distance marks, and buildings as foul lines.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Modern Heritage (1800 to today), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of The Americas: Caribbean.

Kani shawl weaving by talim code

Also known as kani, talim

In the Kashmir valley, weavers build a fine goat-wool shawl on a frame loom with dozens of small wooden bobbins, one for each patch of colour. A coded chart called talim is read aloud row by row, and the pattern grows in the cloth itself.

How to play

  1. Look at the chart card: it shows the motif and thread colour wanted in each square.
  2. Pick a thread colour from the spools, then a motif.
  3. Touch a square to weave it. The shawl is a mirror of itself left to right, so the partner square is woven too.
  4. Press Check to compare your cloth with the chart and earn up to three stars.
Rules at a glance
  • The shawl pattern is symmetric left to right: every stitch you place appears on its mirror square.
  • Placing the same motif and colour again on a square removes it.
  • One colour patch belongs to one bobbin, so each square must match the colour of the chart exactly.
  • Score is the share of squares that match the chart; 98 percent or more earns three stars, 85 percent two, 60 percent one.

About the tradition

Kani weaving comes from Kanihama and nearby villages in Kashmir, India, where weavers, chart writers and dyers each hold a part of the craft. A single shawl can take many months to finish.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of South Asia.

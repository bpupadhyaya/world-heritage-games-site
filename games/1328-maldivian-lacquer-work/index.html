Maldivian lacquer work

Also known as ލިއެލާ ޖެހުން · liyelaa jehun

Maldivian makers turn a wooden vase on a hand lathe and press on sticks of lac in layers of red, black and yellow while it spins. They then scrape back through the layers with a graver to bring out rings and scrolls in the colour beneath. Here you turn, colour and engrave a vase.

How to play

  1. Look at the lit vase in its frame: it shows the shape, the colour of each band and the engraving.
  2. Drag across the spinning wood to set how wide each row is turned.
  3. Tap a band, then tap a lac colour to melt it on, then press Engrave.
  4. Choose the ring or the scroll graver and scrape each lit mark; the colour below shows through.
  5. Press Check to see your stars.
Rules at a glance
  • Shape counts for half of the score, the colour of each band for a quarter and the engraving for a quarter.
  • Each scrape shows the layer beneath, so the order of the colours matters.
  • A ring or scroll counts when it lies within one row of the lit mark; scrapes elsewhere lose points.
  • Undo takes back one stroke; Reglaze goes back to the colours.
  • Three stars for a clean vase.

About the tradition

Lacquerwork has long been a craft of the Maldives. A turned wooden piece is coated with layers of coloured lac as it spins, then engraved through the layers so that bands and scrolls appear in the colours below.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of South Asia.

Bobbin lace making

Also known as Kloppeln · dentelle aux fuseaux

Bobbin lace is made on a firm cushion over a pricked pattern. Pairs of wooden bobbins are crossed and twisted, and each crossing is held with a pin, until plaits, nets and medallions grow into lace.

How to play

  1. Pick a thread from the bobbins and a motif from the tray under the cushion.
  2. Touch a hole in the pricked pattern to work that motif there; it is repeated on the partner holes after each quarter turn.
  3. Copy the pricking shown beside the cushion, then press Check.
Rules at a glance
  • The pattern repeats in quarter turns, so each motif you work is also made on up to three partner holes.
  • A hole counts only when its motif and its thread colour both match the pricking you are copying.
  • Three stars need a nearly perfect copy; you can undo or clear as often as you like.

About the tradition

Pillow lace has been worked in the towns of Flanders, the English Midlands, Normandy and Slovenia for hundreds of years, and lace schools and guilds still teach the bobbin crossings today.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Celtic and Atlantic Europe.

Kamba funnel trumpet

Also known as Maso · so

The Kamba funnel trumpet is a bamboo trumpet of the Akamba, fitted into a calabash funnel as a resonator and played by young men for amusement. A metre-long bamboo tube stopped at the upper end is blown across a rectangular notch, with the calabash funnel at the bottom amplifying the sound.

How to play

  1. Four notes of the trumpet are lit along the tube, the higher ones nearer the notch.
  2. A band of breath runs from the notch to the note you strike, and rings of sound leave the funnel.
  3. Long notes swell slowly: strike close to the beat and let them ring.
Rules at a glance
  • A call is a line of long notes, each struck close to its beat.
  • Long notes swell slowly: strike on the beat and let them ring.
  • The round ends when the last note has been judged; the score is the share of the best possible.

About the tradition

It is a young players' instrument, played for amusement rather than at public events.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Africa: Great Lakes and Indian Ocean.

Lali

The lali is a pair of hollowed logs of Fiji, one large and one small, each with a long narrow slit. Laid side by side and struck with two long beaters, the thick lips give deep strokes and the thin lips bright ones, and the two logs answer each other.

How to play

  1. The two logs lie on a woven mat seen from above: the large one at the top, the small one below it.
  2. Each log has a thick lip for a low tone and a thin lip for a higher tone. Big low and Big high are on the large log, Small low and Small high on the small one.
  3. A ring closes on the place to strike. Strike as it lands.
  4. Begin with the big log alone, then bring in the small log.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Each pattern repeats twice.
  • The patterns count four beats to the bar.

About the tradition

In Fijian villages the lali is beaten to call people together and to mark the start of a gathering. Players learn to make the large and small logs speak in turn, so a short pattern can be heard from one end of the village to the other.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Pacific and Oceania.

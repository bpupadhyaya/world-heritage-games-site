Ravanahatha

The ravanahatha is a bowed fiddle of Rajasthan and western India. A half coconut shell covered with skin forms the round body, and a long bamboo pole forms the neck, ending in two large pegs. Two strings, one of steel and one of horsehair, are played with a bow hung with small bells. It is easy to make and is played by travelling singers.

How to play

  1. Two strings run up the bamboo neck. Each lane marks a string and a place to stop it.
  2. A bead slides up the string to the spot. Press and bow as it lands.
  3. The line is slow and singing, so hold each note until the next one arrives.
  4. Learn the phrase by echo, then play it through with the pulse.
Rules at a glance
  • The scale has seven notes with a flat seventh.
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Each line runs through twice.
  • Lines are written for this game in the style of the tradition.

About the tradition

The ravanahatha is played by travelling singers and storytellers of Rajasthan. The coconut-shell bowl, the long bamboo neck and the small bells on the bow are its signature.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of South Asia.

Kashmiri papier-mache painting

Also known as kari-qalamkar

In Kashmir, boxes, trays and plates are built up from layers of pulped paper, smoothed, lacquered black and then painted by hand. The painter draws fine gold lines first and fills the petals and leaves with colour afterwards.

How to play

  1. Look at the framed plate: that is the finished piece you are painting.
  2. Tap the gold pot to load the pen, then press and drag along the lit guide lines. Move slowly and the gold lies thick; the line thins and breaks as the paint runs out, so dip again.
  3. When the lines are done, press Next. Pick a colour and tap each petal or leaf to flood it.
  4. Press Check when the plate is finished to see your stars.
Rules at a glance
  • The plate has two steps: gold line work first, colour fills second; each step is judged on its own and the two marks are averaged.
  • Line work is judged by how much of each guide line your gold covers, close to the line; paint far from any guide counts against you.
  • Fills are judged by how much of each shape holds the right colour; colour flows out from your tap and stops at the outline.
  • The pattern repeats four times round the centre. Mirror repeats each stroke on the other three quarters, and your marks are rewarded when all four quarters match.
  • The pen holds a limited load of paint: dip again when the line goes dry. Undo takes back the last stroke.
  • The guide can be shown bright, faint or hidden, so you can learn the pattern by heart.
  • Stars: 88 or more gives three, 65 two, 40 one. Doing nothing earns none.

About the tradition

Papier-mache work has been made in the Kashmir valley for centuries, where craftspeople shape the pulp (sakhtsazi) and painters (naqash) decorate it with leaves, flowers and birds in gold and colour. The skill is handed down within families and workshops and is still practised today.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of South Asia.

Uilleann pipes

The uilleann pipes are the bellows-blown bagpipe of Ireland. A small bag is filled by a bellows worked by the elbow instead of the mouth, so the piper can sing or talk while playing. A long chanter with brass keys is played on the lap, and three drones lie across it.

How to play

  1. A covered hole glows; an open hole shows its dark bore. The chanter is the pipe with the finger holes.
  2. The bag gives each time a note sounds, and the drones keep the home note.
  3. Strike close to the beat; a repeated note is cut with a quick grace note.
Rules at a glance
  • Seven notes of the chanter, each struck close to its beat.
  • The drone sounds from the first breath to the last: the notes sit on top of it.
  • The round ends when the last note has been judged; the score is the share of the best possible.

About the tradition

Because the bellows keeps the air steady and sweet, the pipes are heard in slow airs and quick dance tunes alike.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Crossroads and Golden Ages (about 500 to 1500), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Celtic and Atlantic Europe.

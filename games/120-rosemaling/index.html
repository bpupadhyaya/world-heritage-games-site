Rosemaling

Also known as Norwegian decorative painting

Rosemaling is the decorative painting of Norway, in which scrolls, leaves and flowers are brushed in a few quick strokes onto bowls, boxes, chests and walls. Here you decorate a wooden bowl, laying a rosette, C-scrolls and leaves over a deep blue-green ground.

How to play

  1. Study the finished bowl in the frame above the workbench.
  2. Dip the brush in a pot of colour, then press and drag along the lit guide lines. A slow, steady pull lays a fat stroke that thins as the paint runs out.
  3. Dip again whenever the paint is low; the Mirror button repeats your strokes on the other half of the bowl.
  4. Press Next for the cream highlights, then Check to see how closely your painting follows the pattern.
Rules at a glance
  • Each stroke is judged on how closely it follows the guide line and whether it is in the right colour.
  • Shapes such as the rosette petals are judged on how much of each holds the right colour.
  • The bowl pattern is symmetric, so both halves are compared for evenness.
  • Paint outside the guide counts against you; Undo takes back the last stroke.
  • The bowl is painted in two steps: the scrolls, rosette and leaves first, then the cream highlights.

About the tradition

Rosemaling developed in the valleys of Norway from the 1700s, with regional styles such as Telemark, Hallingdal and Rogaland. Painters used a flat brush loaded with colour on one side so a single pull swells and thins, and the craft is kept alive today by painters and museums.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Northern and Eastern Europe.

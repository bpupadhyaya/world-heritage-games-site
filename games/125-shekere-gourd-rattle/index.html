Shekere gourd rattle

Also known as Shekere · Sekere

The shekere is a large dried gourd wrapped in a loose net of beads or seeds. Shaken, the beads rattle; touched with the fingertips they click sharply; struck on the base with the palm the gourd gives a deep, hollow bass. Skilled players toss and spin it to draw layered rhythms from the net.

How to play

  1. Four places on the gourd are lit: Shake high on the neck, Slap and Roll on the belly, and Bass at the base.
  2. A ring closes on a place when its stroke is due. Strike that place just as the ring meets the gourd.
  3. Begin with the four-beat bead pulse, then try the twelve-pulse figure.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Each pattern is played twice through.
  • Patterns are simplified teaching figures in the style of West African gourd-rattle playing, not transcriptions.

About the tradition

The shekere comes from the music of the Yoruba people of West Africa and travelled to the Caribbean and Brazil, where gourd rattles of this kind still join drum ensembles.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin.

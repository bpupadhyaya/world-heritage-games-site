Opa tubular gourd

Also known as Ọpọ́ · Idoma tube gourd

The opa is a long tubular gourd open at both ends, played by Idoma women for their own amusement. The lower end is beaten against the forearm or thigh while the other hand rhythmically covers and uncovers the upper opening, making a pulsing note.

How to play

  1. Three places are lit: Beat on the body of the gourd, Cover at the wide mouth, Open when the mouth is freed.
  2. A ring closes on the place to strike. A pad closes over the mouth on the Cover.
  3. Keep the beat steady and let the mouth make the pulse.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Each pattern repeats twice.
  • The round ends when the last stroke has been judged; the score is the share of the best possible.

About the tradition

It matches the shantu of the Hausa, a similar long tube gourd, and is played for pleasure rather than at public events.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Africa: Sahel, West and Central.

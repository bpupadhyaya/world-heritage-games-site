Shan seed-treadle loom

Also known as Shan treadle weaving · Shan cotton weaving

In the Shan hills of Myanmar, cotton cloth is woven on a heavy loom that stands close to the ground. The weaver works the treadles with her toes; they are often made from large pierced wild-bean seeds. A shuttle of cotton thread crosses the opened threads to make plain, striped and checked cloth.

How to play

  1. Pick a cotton colour from the bobbins and a pattern shape from the tray.
  2. Touch a square on the cloth to weave that shape there; it is mirrored across and down the cloth.
  3. Copy the cloth shown on the card, then press Check.
Rules at a glance
  • The cloth is mirrored both left to right and top to bottom, so each shape is woven on up to four squares.
  • A square counts only when its shape and its cotton colour both match the cloth you are copying.
  • Three stars need a nearly perfect copy; you can undo or clear as often as you like.

About the tradition

The Shan region lies in eastern Myanmar, where families have long woven their own cotton cloth. An early twentieth-century account describes a heavy loom close to the ground with treadles made of large seeds, and a weaver who could finish several yards of plain cloth in a day.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Southeast Asia and the Islands.

Kutch Embroidery

Also known as કચ્છી ભરતકામ

In Kutch, in western India, embroiderers cover dark cotton with loops of bright silk in chain stitch and hold small round mirrors in place with stitched frames. Each community has its own colours and motifs.

How to play

  1. Choose a silk colour and a shape from the tray.
  2. Touch a square to embroider it; the mirror partner on the other half of the cloth is worked too.
  3. Copy the plan beside the cloth, then press Check.
Rules at a glance
  • The panel mirrors left to right, so each shape you place is also worked on its partner square.
  • The round silver shape is a mirror; it is held in by the stitched frame around it.
  • A square counts only when its shape and its silk colour both match the plan; undo and clear as often as you like.

About the tradition

Embroidery of Kutch is made by women of several pastoral and farming communities, who stitch dowry cloths, bags and clothing with their own recognisable motifs. Mirror work reflects light across the cloth and is a signature of the region.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of South Asia.

Ngatu bark cloth making

Also known as koka'anga

Lay strips of soaked mulberry bark on a log anvil, beat them with a wooden beater until they join into one even cloth, then pound it smooth and see its pattern appear.

How to play

  1. Press Lay to put the strips on the log.
  2. Drag the beater across the strips until the cloth is even.
  3. Press Lift.
  4. Pound each zone as it lights.
  5. Press Check.
Rules at a glance
  • The strips overlap, so the cloth is thick where they cross and thin in the gaps.
  • Beating spreads the bark; the score is how even the cloth is.
  • Fibre that is moved stays where the beater leaves it; the heap never gets lighter, so every stroke spreads it further.
  • The form of the sheet counts for 60 points and the finish for 40.
  • Three stars for 88 or more, two stars for 65 and one star for 40. Watch and learn makes a whole sheet for you.

About the tradition

Ngatu is a cloth made in Tonga from the inner bark of the paper mulberry tree. Strips are soaked and beaten on a wooden anvil until they widen and felt together, then pasted into long sheets.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Pacific and Oceania.

Lauhala weaving

Also known as pandanus weaving

Long pandanus leaves are dried, split into even strips and plaited over and under on the slant. Dyed strips run through the pale mat to draw chevrons, diamonds and borders. Copy the pattern, which mirrors left to right.

How to play

  1. Pick a dyed strip colour and a pattern shape.
  2. Place it; the mirror partner is plaited too.
  3. Check the mat against the pattern.
  4. Watch and learn shows a finished round, one placement at a time.
Rules at a glance
  • The mat mirrors left to right.
  • Strip colours must match.
  • Placing the same shape in the same colour again, or choosing the eraser, clears it and its mirror partners.
  • The result compares your piece with the pattern cell by cell: three stars at 98 percent or more, two at 85, one at 60.
  • The pattern to copy is always shown beside the work and as a faint guide on the cloth.

About the tradition

Plaiting of dried pandanus leaf is kept alive across Samoa and the wider Pacific, where mats, baskets and fans are made for daily life and for gifts. Techniques pass from hand to hand within families and villages.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Pacific and Oceania.

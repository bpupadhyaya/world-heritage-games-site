Begena

The begena is a large ten-string box lyre of Ethiopia. Its tall, tapering box of dark wood is edged in gilt, two broad arms rise to a heavy crossbar, and ten strings run over a bridge with small leather rests that give each string a soft, humming buzz. It is played seated, in a slow, even tempo.

How to play

  1. Ten strings run from the crossbar to the bridge, the lowest on the left. Each lane marks a place to pluck a string.
  2. A bead slides down a string. Pluck the lane as it lands.
  3. The tunes are slow, with long, rich notes that ring and buzz.
  4. Count the slow pulse and leave the notes room to sound.
Rules at a glance
  • The ten strings are tuned to a five-note scale across two octaves.
  • Songs are played through twice.
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Songs count four slow beats to the bar.

About the tradition

The begena is a large lyre played in the Ethiopian highlands. Its strings rest on leather pieces that buzz softly when plucked, which gives it its deep, humming tone. Players are often seated and play slowly.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Classical Worlds (about 1000 BCE to 500 CE), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Africa: Nile and the Horn.

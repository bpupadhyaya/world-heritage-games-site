Saz / Bağlama

Also known as Bağlama · Turkish long-neck lute

The saz, also called bağlama, is the long-necked lute of Turkish folk music. Its pear-shaped wooden bowl and slender neck carry three courses of strings, and a row of movable frets lets players reach the small in-between steps of Anatolian melody. Rolling plectrum strokes give it a ringing sound.

How to play

  1. Strings run up a long neck with frets; each lane marks the note on one string and fret.
  2. A bead slides down the string toward its place. Pluck that note just as the bead lands.
  3. Notes marked with a small wave are the in-between steps of the folk scale, a little below the usual pitch.
  4. Try the nine-beat tune first, then the slow melody and the line dance.
Rules at a glance
  • The strings are tuned to D, G and A, and the scale includes one in-between step marked with a wave.
  • The nine-beat tune is counted in groups of two, two, two and three.
  • A pluck on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra plucks cost points.
  • Every tune is played through twice.

About the tradition

For centuries wandering poet-singers called aşıklar have carried the saz from village to village, answering one another in verse. Folk music, line dances and modern bands across Anatolia and beyond all draw on it today.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Crossroads and Golden Ages (about 500 to 1500), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Mesopotamia, Levant and Arabia.

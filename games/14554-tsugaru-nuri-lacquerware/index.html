Tsugaru-nuri lacquerware

Also known as 津軽塗 · Tsugaru nuri · Tsugaru lacquer

Turn a vase, lay coats of dark lac over layers of bright colour, then grind each marked zone with a stone to bring up the mottled colours beneath.

How to play

  1. The vase in its frame shows what to make.
  2. Drag across the wood to set its width, then tap the bands to lay the top coat.
  3. Pick a stone and grind each lit zone: a coarse stone cuts deeper.
  4. Press Check when it matches.
Rules at a glance
  • Shape counts for half the score, the coat on each band for a quarter, and the ground zones for a quarter.
  • A coarse stone reveals the first layer beneath the coat, a fine stone the next.
  • Undo takes back one step.
  • Three stars for 88 or more, two stars for 65 and one star for 40. Watch and learn makes a whole piece for you.

About the tradition

Tsugaru lacquerware from Aomori is made from many layers of coloured lac laid over one another and then ground and polished, so that a mottled pattern of bright colours comes up through a dark ground.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Japan and Korea.

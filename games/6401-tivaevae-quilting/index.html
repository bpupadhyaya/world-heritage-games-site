Tivaevae quilting

Cook Islands tivaevae are quilt tops where big flowers and leaves are cut from coloured cotton and sewn down with tiny blind stitches on white cloth, inside a scalloped border. Copy the pattern, which mirrors both ways.

How to play

  1. Pick a cotton colour and a cut shape.
  2. Sew it down; the mirrored pieces are sewn too.
  3. Check the quilt top against the pattern.
  4. Watch and learn shows a finished round, one placement at a time.
Rules at a glance
  • The quilt mirrors left to right and top to bottom.
  • Cloth colours must match.
  • Placing the same shape in the same colour again, or choosing the eraser, clears it and its mirror partners.
  • The result compares your piece with the pattern cell by cell: three stars at 98 percent or more, two at 85, one at 60.
  • The pattern to copy is always shown beside the work and as a faint guide on the cloth.

About the tradition

Tivaevae are hand-sewn quilt tops of the Cook Islands, made by women's sewing groups and treasured as gifts for birthdays and family occasions. Designs of flowers and leaves are cut freely from bright cotton.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin.

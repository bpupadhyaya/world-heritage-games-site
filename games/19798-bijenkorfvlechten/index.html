Bijenkorfvlechten

Also known as Beehive skep weaving

Bijenkorfvlechten is the Flemish craft of coiling bundles of rye straw into a dome and lashing the rings together with strips of split bramble to make a skep, a traditional home for a colony of honey bees. It is a medieval craft that is still practised in the Kempen, where long rye straw is preferred.

How to play

  1. The lit case above the bench shows the skep as it should finish. Look at how its pattern climbs the curve.
  2. Choose a strand colour below the basket, then lay each stitch across the front of the ring. The gold ring marks the next place and the strip under the basket shows the row in hand.
  3. Work up from the base, one ring at a time, so each ring lines up with the spiral in the case.
  4. Press Check when every stitch is laid. Undo takes the last stitch back.
Rules at a glance
  • The skep is built from 9 rings of 14 lashings, laid from the base upward, one ring at a time. The doorway and crown are already made.
  • Each lashing takes one of three colours: pale straw, dark bramble or smoked russet.
  • The score is the share of stitches whose colour matches the pattern, counted beyond what laying only the commonest colour would earn. Three stars need 88, two need 65 and one needs 40.
  • Undo takes back the last lashing. A skep left plain earns no stars.

About the tradition

Skep makers in the Kempen and elsewhere in Flanders keep the craft alive through workshops and heritage groups, and the craft is documented in Flanders' register of living heritage. A good skep is dense enough to keep out the rain and light enough to carry.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Celtic and Atlantic Europe.

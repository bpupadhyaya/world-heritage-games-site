Tablet weaving

Tablet weaving uses small square cards, each with a hole at every corner and a thread through each hole. Turn the whole set a quarter turn and the threads twist into a new row, so a narrow, strong band grows with a pattern that depends on how the cards are threaded and turned.

How to play

  1. Pick a thread colour from the cones and a pattern shape from the tray.
  2. Touch a square on the band to weave that shape there; it is mirrored on the other half of the band.
  3. Copy the pattern shown on the card, then press Check.
Rules at a glance
  • The band is mirrored left and right, so every shape you weave is also woven on its partner square.
  • A square counts only when its shape and its thread colour both match the pattern you are copying.
  • Three stars need a nearly perfect copy; you can undo or clear as often as you like.

About the tradition

Bands woven with tablets turn up in Iron Age graves in central Europe and in Viking-age finds in Norway, and the craft is still practised in Finland and across Europe for belts, straps and trims. The tools are cheap and portable, which is a large part of why it has lasted.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin.

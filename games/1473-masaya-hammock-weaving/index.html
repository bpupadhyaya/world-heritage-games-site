Masaya Hammock Weaving

Also known as Hamaca de Masaya

A hammock is made of cotton cord stretched over a wide frame and knotted row after row into an open net. Bands of bright colour are chosen to make the pattern, and the two ends are gathered into strong loops for hanging. Masaya in Nicaragua is known for its hammock workshops.

How to play

  1. Pick a cord colour from the skeins and a pattern shape from the tray.
  2. Touch a square on the net to knot that shape there; it is mirrored left to right.
  3. Copy the pattern shown on the card, then press Check.
Rules at a glance
  • The pattern is mirrored left to right, so each shape is knotted on up to two squares.
  • A square counts only when its shape and its cord colour both match the pattern you are copying.
  • Three stars need a nearly perfect copy; you can undo or clear as often as you like.

About the tradition

Masaya is a craft town in Nicaragua whose workshops are well known for hammocks, and hammocks themselves have been used across Central America and the Caribbean for centuries. The cord is pulled over a frame, knotted by hand and striped in the colours a customer asks for.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of The Americas: Mesoamerica.

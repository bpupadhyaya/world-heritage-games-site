Mokoro polling

Also known as Mokoro polling (Okavango dugout race) · Okavango dugout race

A slim dugout on a channel of reeds and water lilies: stand at the stern and push the pole, left then right, to keep the line straight.

How to play

  1. Watch the ring close on the pad and press the moment it meets the pad.
  2. Alternate sides with every beat; two pushes on one side turn you off line.
  3. Keep the rhythm through the shallow patch and past the marker.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat gives a full push; off-beat strokes spend strength and give almost nothing.
  • Rough water shrinks the timing window.
  • Stars: first with a steady rhythm for three; first alone for two; on the podium for one.

About the tradition

The mokoro is a dugout poled through the shallow channels of the Okavango delta. The poler stands at the stern and sets the pace with a long push on every stroke.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Age of Contact and Craft (about 1500 to 1800), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Africa: Southern Africa.

Family Island Regatta

Also known as Bahamian sloop racing

Wooden working sloops with a huge mainsail and crews hiking out on planks: keep the sail trimmed to the gold marker and hold your lead down the course.

How to play

  1. Hold the left or the right side to ease or haul the sail.
  2. Keep the white dot on the gold marker: it follows the wind as it shifts.
  3. Stay on the marker as you round the buoys.
Rules at a glance
  • Speed comes from how well the sail matches the wind.
  • The current gives every boat a small push.
  • Stars: first with a steady trim for three; first alone for two; on the podium for one.

About the tradition

Each year island crews race locally built wooden sloops with long booms and tall sails, hanging out over the side on planks to hold the boat upright.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Age of Contact and Craft (about 1500 to 1800), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of The Americas: Caribbean.

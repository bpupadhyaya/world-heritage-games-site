Kamba musya bell dance

Also known as Musya

A dance of Akamba youth in Kenya in which dancers bind small iron bells around one leg and stamp the rhythm, moving in twisting lines and rounds.

How to play

  1. Four places are lit beside the dancer: Stamp, Step, Sway and Shake.
  2. A ring closes on a place when that movement is due. Tap the place as the ring closes and the dancer answers; the bells ring hardest on the stamp and the shake.
  3. Start with the stamps and steps, then the twisting line and the round.
Rules at a glance
  • A tap right on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and taps with no ring open cost points.
  • Each pattern is played twice through; every pattern is counted in fours.
  • Patterns are simplified teaching figures in the style of the dance, not choreography to copy.

About the tradition

In an afternoon dance of the Akamba, young men wind many small iron bells around one leg so that every stamp becomes part of the music. They dance in lines that twist and in circles, and the bells keep the rhythm for the singers.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Africa: Great Lakes and Indian Ocean.

Madal drum

Also known as मादल

The madal is a two-headed barrel drum of the hills of Nepal. It rests across the lap or hangs at the waist and is played on both heads with the palms and fingertips. The right head carries a disc of black paste that gives a clear ring, while a lump of soft dough pressed on the left head makes a deep, short bass.

How to play

  1. Four places on the drum are marked: the bass head on the left, the centre and the edge of the pasted head on the right, and the wooden shell between them.
  2. A ring closes on a place when its stroke is due. Strike that place just as the ring meets the skin.
  3. Begin with the slow hill-song pulse, where the bass answers two light strokes of the right head, then move to the longer dance figure.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • The shell is a light knuckle knock on the wood and works as an accent.
  • Each pattern is played twice through.
  • Patterns are simplified teaching figures in the style of Nepali hill songs, not transcriptions.

About the tradition

The madal is heard with hill songs and village dancing across Nepal. Its pasted head is built up in thin layers by the maker, which is what gives the centre its clear, ringing voice.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of South Asia.

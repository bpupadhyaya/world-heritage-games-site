Surdo

The surdo is the big bass drum that holds a samba band together. A felt mallet strikes the head for an open boom while the other hand presses the skin to mute it, so the drum answers itself in a steady one-two heartbeat.

How to play

  1. The drum lies on its side; its wide head faces you.
  2. The centre gives the open boom, the lower part the muted answer, and the outer ring a short edge tone.
  3. A ring closes on the place to strike. Strike as it lands.
  4. Begin with the steady two-beat heartbeat, then add the edge and rim strokes.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Each pattern repeats twice.
  • The patterns count two beats to the bar, played in sixteenth notes.

About the tradition

In a Brazilian samba band the surdo plays the pulse: a muted stroke on the first beat and an open boom on the second, so every dancer and drummer knows where the beat lies.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Age of Contact and Craft (about 1500 to 1800), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of The Americas: Andes and South America.

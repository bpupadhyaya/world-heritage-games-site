Kissar

The kissar is the bowl lyre of the Nile valley. A round wooden bowl is covered with hide and laced at the edge; two slim arms rise from it and carry a crossbar, and five strings run from leather-wrapped ties on the bar down over a bridge to a tail cord. It is plucked with the fingers to accompany songs.

How to play

  1. Five strings run from the crossbar down to the bridge, the lowest on the left. Each lane marks a place to pluck a string.
  2. A bead slides down a string. Pluck the lane just as the bead lands on it.
  3. The tunes are built from the five notes of the scale in short phrases that answer one another.
  4. Learn a phrase first by echo, then play it along with the pulse.
Rules at a glance
  • The five strings are tuned to a five-note scale with no half steps.
  • Songs are played through twice.
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • The phrases count four beats to the bar.

About the tradition

Bowl lyres have been made along the Nile for thousands of years. The kissar is still played in Nubian villages, where the bowl is often carved from a single piece of wood and covered with fresh hide each season.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of Classical Worlds (about 1000 BCE to 500 CE), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of Africa: Nile and the Horn.

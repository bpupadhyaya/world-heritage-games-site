Mesob basket weaving

Also known as Mesob

The mesob is a lidded woven table from Ethiopia: a wide-bodied basket on a foot with a tall cone-shaped cover, used to keep and serve flatbread. Grass and palm leaf, partly dyed, are coiled upward and bound in rings of red, green and gold, with eye and zigzag patterns set into the wall.

How to play

  1. The lit case above the bench shows the mesob as it should finish. Look at how its pattern climbs the curve.
  2. Choose a strand colour below the basket, then lay each stitch across the front of the ring. The gold ring marks the next place and the strip under the basket shows the row in hand.
  3. Work up from the base, one ring at a time, so each ring lines up with the eyes in the case.
  4. Press Check when every stitch is laid. Undo takes the last stitch back.
Rules at a glance
  • The mesob is built from 5 rings of 15 stitches on the wide body, laid from the foot upward. The foot, shoulder and cone cover are already woven.
  • Each stitch takes one of four colours: straw, red, green or gold.
  • The score is the share of stitches whose colour matches the pattern, counted beyond what laying only the commonest colour would earn. Three stars need 88, two need 65 and one needs 40.
  • Undo takes back the last stitch. A mesob left plain earns no stars.

About the tradition

Mesob belong to the wider basketry of the Harari people of Harar and the Ethiopian highlands, and the Harar mesob is prized for its dense, intricate patterns. Weavers, mostly women, still make them for homes and markets, and the shape has come to stand for Ethiopian food and hospitality.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Africa: Nile and the Horn.

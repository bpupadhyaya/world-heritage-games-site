Darbuka Iqa'at Rhythm Drum

Also known as darbuka · doumbek, iqa'at

The darbuka is a goblet-shaped hand drum of Egypt, the Levant and Turkey, today usually pressed from metal. Held across the lap, it is played with both hands: the deep Dum in the middle of the head, the sharp Tek with the fingertips near the edge, and the light Ka with the other hand.

How to play

  1. Three places on the head are lit: the centre for Dum, an inner ring for Tek and an outer ring for Ka. The hoop makes a Rim click.
  2. A ring closes on the place to strike. The rhythms are played as a chain of Dum, Tek and Ka.
  3. The cycles are called iqa'at: maqsum is the best known.
  4. Say the names Dum, Tek and Ka as you play, in time with the lit places.
Rules at a glance
  • A stroke on the beat is perfect, a near one is good, and extra strokes cost points.
  • Each pattern repeats twice.
  • The three cycles are played in turn, each with its own count of beats to the bar.

About the tradition

Egyptian and Levantine players name their cycles, called iqa'at, by the arrangement of Dum and Tek. Maqsum and baladi are heard in dance music from Cairo to Beirut.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Africa: Nile and the Horn.

Adinkra Cloth Stamping

Also known as Adinkra

Adinkra cloth is printed by hand in Ghana: the maker rules the cloth into squares with a comb dipped in dye, then presses carved calabash stamps in dark dye boiled from tree bark, one design to a square. Spirals, rings, stars, chain links and bands of stripes fill the cloth row by row.

How to play

  1. Pick a dye colour and a stamp shape from the tray under the cloth.
  2. Tap a square to press the stamp there; tap again with the same stamp to lift it off.
  3. Copy the small cloth shown above, then press Check.
Rules at a glance
  • Every square is stamped on its own: the cloth has no mirror, so each square is worked separately.
  • A square counts only when its stamp shape and its dye colour both match the target.
  • Three stars need a nearly perfect copy; you can undo or clear as often as you like.

About the tradition

Adinkra printing is practised in Ghana, with a centre at Ntonso near Kumasi, and in Cote d'Ivoire. Printers carve their own stamps from calabash and cook the dye from bark, and cloth was traditionally printed on hand-woven cotton.

Origins

A living tradition passed from person to person; it is not tied to a single dated origin. It belongs to the cultural world of Africa: Sahel, West and Central.

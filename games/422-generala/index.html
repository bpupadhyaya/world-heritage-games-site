Generala

Also known as five-dice cup game

A five-dice cup game of the Rio de la Plata. Shake the cup, throw up to three times a turn, keep the dice you like, and write each result in one of ten boxes: numbers, a run of five, a full house, four alike or five alike.

How to play

  1. Shake the cup and throw the five dice.
  2. Keep any dice you like by sliding them into the rack, then throw the others again, up to three throws in all.
  3. Choose a box on the sheet for the dice you finished with; each box is used once.
  4. A box you cannot fill is crossed out for nothing. When all ten boxes are written the higher total wins.
Rules at a glance
  • Each turn you may throw the five dice up to three times, keeping any dice you like between throws.
  • Numbers 1 to 6: add up the dice showing that number. Escalera (five in a row; 3-4-5-6-1 counts) scores 20, Full (three and two) 30, Poker (four alike) 40, Generala (five alike) 50.
  • Escalera, Full and Poker made on the very first throw (servida) score 5 extra.
  • A Generala made on the first throw wins the whole game.
  • A second Generala after you already hold 50 in the Generala box raises that box to 100 (Generala doble).
  • When no box fits you must cross one out for nothing. Ten boxes each; the higher total wins.

About the tradition

Generala is a family game of Argentina, Uruguay and Paraguay, played around kitchen tables with a leather cup and five dice, with the old combination names Escalera, Full and Poker. Rules differ from house to house; this version follows the common ten-box form.

Origins

Usually placed in the era of River Valleys and First Cities (about 3000 to 1000 BCE), although its exact origins are uncertain, within the cultural world of The Americas: Andes and South America.
